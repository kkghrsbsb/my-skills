# Research Orient

Read [the shared protocol](../../research-flow/protocol.md). Start with the user's actual question and intended use, not an exhaustive inventory of every document.

Announce `orient`, whether the pass is read-only, and whether it remains in the main conversation. Inspect only enough project instructions, existing artifacts, source lists, and representative materials to identify where relevant knowledge lives. If `.agents/research-flow/understanding.md` exists, treat it as a lead and verify important claims against actual materials.

Clarify or propose the smallest useful question. Before giving a full explanation, ask the researcher one or two open questions about their current model and confidence when those answers will help set the starting depth. Use questions they have already asked as evidence of what needs explaining; do not demand a formal test or infer understanding from their having read a summary. Identify the audience, decision or learning goal, scope and date boundary, accessible sources, and the most important unknowns. Distinguish an observed fact from an old summary, a source claim, and the user's stated goal.

Return a compact orientation brief: the working question, the researcher's expressed starting understanding and gaps, relevant material map, high-value first sources, and likely limitations. Do not create an understanding file, collect new sources, or edit notes during a read-only orientation unless those actions are in the user's request.
